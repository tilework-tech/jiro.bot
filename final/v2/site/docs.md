# Noridoc: site

Path: @/final/v2/site

### Overview

- This is the Vite + TypeScript scroll site for Jiro.bot v2. It is one tall page of pixel-art stops and joining bands, drawn on Canvas 2D under real HTML copy, with no WebGL.
- A single conveyor belt runs through the whole page on a fixed full-viewport canvas. The belt's behaviour lives in a pure, seedable model in `@/final/v2/site/src/belt`, and a separate renderer draws it.
- All seven stops (hero → product → compare → table → FAQ → price → pond) and the six bands between them are built and mounted: `BUILT` in `@/final/v2/site/src/main.ts` lists every stop in `@/final/v2/site/src/layout.ts`. The belt ends at the pond, where a koi leaps over it and eats what is on the plates.
- Scrolling is magnetic: the page only rests on a stop (or inside one taller than the screen), and glides between stops while the belt runs half again as fast (`@/final/v2/site/src/belt/snap.ts`, `@/final/v2/site/src/main.ts`).
- Two games play in place in a same-origin iframe: Sushi Rush in the stop-4 arcade cabinet and Daily Roll in the pond's yatai stall (`@/final/v2/site/src/cabinet.ts` on the page, `public/games/cabinet/` inside the frame).
- Scenes are not only sprite loops: procedural ambient effects (lantern glows, fireflies, pond ripples, rain, steam, drips, fish shadows) are drawn over each scene's art by `@/final/v2/site/src/fx.ts`, and people make small idle moves by themselves.
- `public/still/` is a static no-JavaScript version of the whole journey, generated from the same copy at build time.

### How it fits into the larger codebase

- Art comes from the pipeline in `@/final/v2/tools`, described in `@/final/v2/art/README.md`. The site reads only exported files:
  - `public/art/<scene>/scene.json`, with its base PNG and sprite strips (scenes are the stops plus `band<N>`)
  - `public/art/belt/`: tile, plates, items and `items/frames.json`
- Each `scene.json` carries `size` in world units, layers and sprites with their `grain`, loop durations, click reactions (a `-react` frame strip, or a `motion` block naming a cut-out and a background patch), `idle` moves on sprites (a motion block with no `kind` when the sprite only moves by itself), `fx` (procedural effects in world units), `surfaces` (where plates can be set down), `water` (pond only: where a dropped plate goes to the koi) and `eggs` (click areas and speech lines). The site has no per-scene drawing code: everything a scene does is data in its `scene.json`.
- `@/final/v2/site/src/content.ts` and `public/games/` are copied from PR #13's `@/final/site`, including the scripted rate-limit replays used in the compare stop and the shared game engine. `public/games/cabinet/` is v2's own embed page around that engine. Product facts come from noriagentic.com and are sourced in `@/final/site/docs/CONTENT-SOURCES.md` (PR #13's file, not edited here); the stop-4 table was refreshed from live noriagentic.com on 2026-10-01 and the FAQ on 2026-10-02 (see Table stop and FAQ stop), so for those two this file is the newer source. The visual rules come from `@/final/v2/DESIGN-BRIEF.md`; the resolution changes and the stop-3 shift are planned in `@/final/v2/PLAN-R2.md` and `@/final/v2/PLAN-R3.md`, stop 4 in `@/final/v2/PLAN-S4.md`, stops 5–7 with bands 3–5 in `@/final/v2/PLAN-FINAL.md`, round 6 (belt speed and fill, magnetic scroll, moving click reactions, board and price-tag restyle) in `@/final/v2/PLAN-R6.md`, and round 7 (plate effects and belt drops, wider surfaces, procedural effects replacing light sprites, idle people) in `@/final/v2/PLAN-R7.md`.
- `@/final/v2/site/tests/art` reads `public/art` and checks it against `@/final/v2/palette/jiro56.gpl`, so a re-export is checked on the next `npm test`. It also asserts that `public/games/cabinet/jiro56.gpl` is byte-identical to the master palette, so the game and the art snap to the same colours. It also checks resolution: room layers at ≥4 art px per world unit, egg/trigger/character sprites at grain ≥8 with strips exactly `w × grain` by `h × grain`, plates ≥120 px opaque, items between 56 px and 70% of a plate.
- `@/final/v2/site/tools/capture.mjs` and the Playwright e2e suite drive the page through `window.__jiro`, a read-only test hook set once boot finishes. It exposes:
  - a `ready` flag
  - belt speed and rest speed
  - the plates in view (each with its item's drawn `angle`) and slots in view, and the active effects
  - `koi()`: running counts of leaps, items eaten and plates fed to the koi
  - `fx(sceneId)`: a scene's effect state, `{swarming, kinds}` (whether any fireflies are swarming, and the effect kinds it draws)
  - `surface(id)`: the viewport position and page-px `w`/`h` of a named surface or water rect (for example `pond-water`); it throws on an unknown id
- `npm run build` runs `tools/build-still.mjs` before `tsc` and `vite build`. That script regex-extracts the comparison table, FAQ and plans from `src/content.ts` (it never imports it) and writes `public/still/index.html`: each stop's `art/<scene>/base.png` with its copy as plain HTML, linked from the pond stop. It throws if it finds fewer than six FAQ entries, other than four plans or fewer than five table rows, so a reshaped `content.ts` breaks the build rather than the still page.
- `serve.mjs` is a static server over `dist/`. It serves byte ranges for Safari, writes an access log, and accepts `/__diag` beacons.

### Core Implementation

**World and layout** (`layout.ts`). Everything is placed in world units:

- The world is 360 units wide.
- Each stop is 202 units tall.
- Each stop except the pond is followed by a band with its own height (`BANDS` = 153, 86, 100, 110, 108, 125). The hero's crawlspace band is the tallest. Each band's height is set by its art: the band base is 1440 px wide and 4 art px per unit tall, so band 2 grew from 50 to 100 for its floor-slab art, and bands 3–5 (storage cutaway, drain, garden wall) came out at 110, 108 and 125 instead of the planned 80, 80 and 100.

`stopTop` and `regionAtY` map world y to a stop or a band. Art density is separate from these units (see Scenes), so changing a grain never moves the route, surfaces or eggs.

**Page mapping** (`main.ts`). World coordinates map to the page as `s = innerWidth / 360` CSS px per world unit.

- On narrow screens (≤760 px) each stop's `.copy` block sits above its scene canvas. Every copy height above a world y is added to the page y, so the belt route, surfaces and egg buttons all shift with the copy.
- `pageToWorld` inverts this mapping iteratively.
- Stops, bands and the stage height are absolutely positioned by `layout()`.

**Belt model** (`belt/`). All modules here are pure and unit tested.

| Module | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `route.ts` | A polyline (`routePoints`) with every corner filleted (`BEND_R`). It is sampled as a pose (x, y, heading) by arc length, and has `HIDDEN` rects where architecture covers the belt. A built route exposes those rects (`hidden`), `isHidden(p)` (a point is covered) and `isBuried(p, r)` (a square of half-size `r` around `p` is entirely covered). It runs from the kitchen hatch, along the hero diagonal (measured from the hero art), down behind the crawlspace beam, along the crawlspace floor, down the left edge into the compare stop's counter channel near the bottom of that stop (`COMPARE_CHANNEL`, measured from the compare art), out through the right wall, then straight down x = 320 (`R`) through band 2, the table stop, band 3, the FAQ, band 4, the street and band 5 inside the steel shaft painted at the right edge of each, and finally makes one 90° turn at the pond onto the trestle (`POND_TRESTLE` = 93.75/202 of the stop, measured from the pond art) and runs left to x = −60, past the left edge of the world. Two pond `HIDDEN` rects cover the descent behind the garden wall, bridge and stall (x 248–382, from the pond top to just below the trestle line, so the turn is hidden too) and the trestle's far end (x < 6). `R` was 338 before stop 4; it moved so the visible run sits in the shaft art. |
| `stream.ts` | Slot `i`'s content is a pure function of `(seed, i)`. Two two-state chains decide it: one puts plates on about half the slots and is biased against long runs so bare belt shows between them; the other puts items on about 70% of those plates, in natural runs (about a third of all slots carry food; round 6 raised it from about half of the plates when Martin asked for roughly 40% more food). Items are 70% food, 20% odd items, 10% living food. The rim is grey or blue at random per slot. Items sit 1–3 offset units off-centre and carry an effect index. |
| `slats.ts` | `slatRows`: one row of the tile's period per sample step, placed at travel-shifted arc positions so slats move by the exact belt travel (sub-pixel included) instead of hopping a row at a time. |
| `motion.ts` | `createJourney({restSpeed})` (default 6 units/s). The belt crawls at rest speed forever; `glide(true)` eases it toward 1.5× rest while the page glides between scenes, and `glide(false)` eases it back. Speed is clamped between 25% of rest and 1.5× rest, so it never runs backwards. It owns no scroll state: the page decides when a glide starts and ends. |
| `snap.ts` | Where the page may rest. A `StopSpan` is a stop's page `top`/`bottom`; its resting range is `[top, bottom − viewport]`, which collapses to just `top` when the stop fits on screen. `wheelTarget(y, dir, stops, vh)` returns the next (or previous) resting place, or `null` when the page should scroll natively (inside a stop taller than the screen, or nothing further that way). `glideTarget(y, dir, …)` returns `null` if `y` already rests, otherwise the nearest resting place in the scroll direction (the nearest overall when `dir` is 0), except that an overshoot of under 20% of a screen past a tall stop's end settles back onto it. Bands are never resting places. |
| `events.ts` | At most two rare events per visit, seeded and spaced out: walk-and-cuddle and fall-off. Each fires only when suitable plates are in view. |
| `drop.ts` | `resolveDrop(p, surfaces, water, onBelt)` resolves a drop point. `onBelt` (the renderer found a free belt spot under the drop) wins and means `belt`; otherwise inside a surface means `placed`, inside water means `koi`, and anywhere else means `return`. The only water is the pond scene's `water` rect. |
| `effects.ts` | `plateEffect(i)` maps a plate's seeded effect index to a named reaction and its duration. Indices with `i % 5` of 0 or 2 (about 40%) `explode`; the rest cycle through twelve gags (hop, spin, wobble, squash, puff, sparkle, flip, float, shiver, grow, peek, bounce), indexed so every gag comes round. |
| `koi.ts` | `koiPose(leap, t)`: a straight run from `from` to `to` lifted by a parabola `height` tall, with the nose's heading. `createKoiSchedule({reduced, first, every})`: `tick(dt, inView)` returns true when the koi should leap, `first` s after the pond comes into view, then every `every` s while it stays; leaving resets the wait to at least `first`, and reduced motion never leaps. |
| `menu.ts`, `rng.ts` | The item catalogue by category, plus `hash01` and `mulberry32` |

**Belt renderer** (`beltView.ts`). The `#belt` canvas is fixed to the viewport at device resolution. All belt art is grain 8 (`BELT_GRAIN`): one belt art px is `s / 8` CSS px (`hp`, half a CSS px at 1440 wide, so one device px on a 2× retina screen), and `k` is device px per belt art px. Motion and layout constants (effect offsets, particle speeds and gravity, pick radius, fall physics, the lifted-plate shadow) are authored at grain 4 and multiplied by `U = BELT_GRAIN / 4`, so changing the grain keeps their on-screen size.

- **Slats.** Route poses are tabled once per slat sample of arc and interpolated, so slats and plates can sit between pixels. `rowStride()` picks how many tile rows one sample covers: the largest divisor `n` of the tile height with `n · k ≤ 1`, so a 1× screen or a phone draws about one slat row per device pixel instead of every grain-8 row. Each sample draws an `n`-row band of the tile (one row taller than its step, so bands overlap), rotated to the heading. Without the stride, grain-8 slats halved the frame rate on the Chromium phone profile, and the 50 ms `dt` clamp in the frame loop made the belt visibly slow down. In the hero the belt bed is already painted into the scene art, so only the tile's bed columns (`TILE_BED`) are overdrawn there (`bedOnly`).
- **Belt ends.** Slat rows and belt plates are skipped only when `isBuried` (the slat test reaches half a belt width plus one sample step; the plate test reaches a plate's size), not when their centre is hidden. After the belt and its plates are drawn, every hidden rect is erased from the belt canvas with `destination-out`. The belt and any plate on it therefore end in a straight horizontal (or rect-edge) cut wherever architecture covers them (kitchen hatch, the floor and beam at the hero bottom, the dining-room walls and floor, the pond's garden wall and the trestle's far end) instead of a jagged row-by-row edge at the belt's angle. Martin asked for belt ends to "always finish horizontally, no matter what the angle of the belt". The walking item of the cuddle event is drawn before the erase; falling and held plates, the koi and particles are drawn after it, so they show over architecture.
- **Smoothing.** Slats are always drawn unsmoothed. Plates and items are drawn with image smoothing on when `k < 1` (art finer than the device), so they average down instead of dropping pixels; smoothing is switched off again after each plate.
- **Slots.** Slots are spaced every `SLOT` world units. Slot position is `travel + PREFILL − i·SLOT`; `PREFILL` keeps the belt populated from the first frame and keeps visible slot indices positive. Plate positions are not rounded, so plates glide.
- **Item offsets.** Stream offsets are 1–3 grain-2 px (half a world unit each) and are scaled by `OFFSET_PX` (half the belt grain) into belt art px.
- **Item rotation.** Items are always drawn upright (`itemAngle` is 0) whichever way the belt runs; only a plate's own click effect (for example spin) turns its item briefly. Plates are never rotated. Martin asked for food to "always be vertical"; before round 6 items turned with the belt heading everywhere except the hero run.
- **Plate state.** The stream says what a slot was born with; `overrides` (a map from slot index) says what the visitor and the events did to it: `gone` (taken off), `walking` and `guest` (the cuddle event), `eaten` (the koi took the item) and `moved` (a plate put there from elsewhere, carrying its own `item` or `null` and `rim`). `slotPlate(i)` combines the two and is the single answer to "what plate, if any, is on slot i now"; drawing, picking, `platesInView` and `slotsInView` all go through it. `isOccupied(i)` (food the koi, the rare events and the counters can see) counts a `moved` plate with food as well as untouched stream food.
- **Interactions.**
  - Clicking a plate plays its `plateEffect`: an explosion (1.4 s: the item bursts into 70 particles of its own colours plus sparkles, vanishes, then grows back from 60% size) or one of the gags, drawn by a switch on the effect name in `drawPlate`, with particles for puff, sparkle and grow and sometimes a quip. Clicking an empty plate spins the plate image. Every click starts an effect, so a plate always reacts.
  - Dragging works with the mouse past 5 px of movement, or after a 260 ms long-press on touch. Only plates with food can be dragged; empty plates only spin.
  - `held.prev` remembers the slot's override at pick-up. Letting go nowhere useful (or cancelling) puts the plate back exactly as it was, so a moved plate stays moved.
  - **Back on the belt.** `beltSpot(page)` finds the nearest visible slot within ¾ of a slot spacing of the drop that is bare belt or carries an empty plate (never the plate's own slot). Dropping there sets a `moved` override on that slot, so the plate rides on from where it was put. If an empty plate was there and the dragged plate came off the belt, the empty plate swaps back to the dragged plate's old slot; otherwise the old slot is `gone`.
  - A plate set on a surface becomes a DOM `<button class="placed-plate">` in `#placed`, which can be dragged again; its slot is never refilled.
  - A plate dropped in the pond water is gone for good (a `gone` override and a splash), finds the `koi-fed` egg and, unless motion is reduced, makes the koi leap at the drop point.
- **Rare events.** These are animated here, and they report egg ids.
- **Koi.** The koi is a grain-8 sprite (`art/belt/koi.png`) drawn on the belt canvas after the hidden-rect erase, so it shows over the trestle and the plates. `koiLine()` (from `main.ts`) is the page y of the trestle line. Each frame `createKoiSchedule` (first leap 3 s after the line is at least 60 px inside the viewport, then every 25 s) may call `leapAt(fullestRun(...))`. Only one leap runs at a time.
  - **Aim.** `fullestRun` takes the trestle slots with food that are visible (not behind a hidden rect), within 4 units of the line and inside the koi's reach, and returns the x with the most such neighbours within `BITE` (34 units). With none in reach the koi does not leap: the schedule's `retry(1.5)` looks again 1.5 s later, so it never jumps at bare belt (on phones only a short stretch of trestle is in reach). `leapAt(target)` builds a 2 s leap 90 units long from below the line on the right to below the line on the left, 80 units high, centred `DESCENT` (27.1 units) right of `target`: on this arc the koi falls back through the line at t ≈ 0.80, which puts the descent on `target`. The centre is clamped to `REACH` (world x 50–160) so both ends of the leap stay in the painted water. The koi is drawn at 80% of its sprite size.
  - **Bite.** Only on the way down (t > 0.5) and only plates that are not hidden: every item within `BITE` of the koi while it is within 30 units of the line becomes `eaten`.
  - **Eating.** While the koi is within 30 units of the line (on the way up and the way down) every occupied slot on the line within 34 units of it that is untouched or `moved` loses its item: an untouched slot gets an `eaten` override (drawn as an empty plate), and a `moved` plate keeps its place and rim with its item set to `null`. The plates roll on without their items, and eaten slots are never refilled. `fullestRun` counts moved plates with food too.
  - Each leap finds the `koi-leap` egg and splashes (steam-style particle bursts) as the koi leaves and re-enters the water. Reduced motion disables both the schedule and the leap on feeding.
  - The aim exists because Martin asked for the koi to "eat a whole lot of the belt": an unaimed leap at a random x often came down on bare belt, especially on phones, so the bite was also widened.

**Scenes** (`scene.ts`). Each built stop and band has its own canvas. Its backing density `G` (canvas px per world unit) is chosen per device by `sceneDensity(cssPerUnit, dpr)`: the smallest of 2, 4 or 8 that covers `CSS px per world unit × devicePixelRatio`. `main.ts` computes it once at boot from `innerWidth / WORLD_W` and passes it to every `mountScene`. At 1440 wide a 1× screen gets 4 and a 2× retina screen gets 8; phones get 4.

| Art grain vs `G` | What happens at load | Per-frame draw |
| --- | --- | --- |
| grain ≤ `G` (e.g. grain-4 rooms on an 8× canvas) | image kept as is | scaled up by `G / grain`, unsmoothed |
| grain > `G` (e.g. grain-8 sprites on a 4× canvas) | averaged down once into an offscreen canvas with high-quality smoothing (`toDensity`) | 1:1 copy |

Downsampling once at load keeps every per-frame draw a plain copy, and a 1× screen shows grain-8 detail as a sharp half-size illustration rather than hard pixels. Memory grows with `G`: an 8× stop canvas is 2880 × 1616 (about 19 MB), so every stop and band canvas on a retina screen together would hold about 200 MB if every one stayed allocated.

- **Release and restore.** Every frame, `main.ts` measures each stop and band canvas's distance from the viewport. Beyond 1.5 viewport heights the scene is `release()`d: its canvas is set to 1 × 1, which frees the backing store, and `draw` becomes a no-op. Within one viewport height it is `restore()`d: the canvas is reallocated at `W·G × H·G` and the frame signature is reset so the next draw repaints it fully. The gap between the two thresholds keeps a canvas from flapping at the boundary. Decoded sprite images and the downsampled offscreen copies stay in memory; only the visible canvas is released.

- Ambient sprite loops run on one shared clock, `performance.now()`. Single-frame ambient sprites exist only to show a detail object at its own grain at rest. No light is a frame-swapped sprite any more (round 7): lanterns, lamps, the candle and the fireflies are procedural effects (see Ambient effects).
- **Click reactions** (`poke`). A sprite with a `motion` block moves the drawn object itself; any other triggered sprite plays its `-react` strip once. Since round 6 almost every creature and prop moves (Martin found the old swapped-in second picture crude); only state changes keep frame strips: Jiro's jaw and blink and the street's traffic light. Clicking an object that is already making the same move does not restart it, and a frame reaction (Jiro's jaw) cancels an idle move on the same sprite. `hasReaction` is true only for frame reactions and motion blocks with a `kind`, so an idle-only sprite is not clickable for a move.
  - **Moving reactions.** At load each motion sprite also loads its `cut` (frame 0 with only the subject opaque) and `under` (the background behind the subject, opaque only over its slightly dilated silhouette), both downsampled to `G` like any sprite. A click (ignored under reduced motion or while the move is playing) records a start time; `motionPose(kind, t)` gives the pose for `MOTION_MS[kind]`: `hop` crouches, springs 9 units up and lands with a squash; `stretch` rises 5 units and stretches 22% longer (the sleeping cats); `wobble` rocks ±0.16 rad about the base, decaying; `swing` rocks ±0.2 rad about the top (hanging things). Poses pivot on the `bottom` or `top` point exported with the sprite.
  - **Idle moves.** A sprite with `idle` `{kind, every, offset}` (`nod`, `breathe` or `sway`) starts that move by itself whenever `(now + offset) % every` falls inside the move's duration (`MOTION_MS`: nod 2.6 s, breathe 3.2 s, sway 4.2 s), using the same cut-out and patch as a click move; a nod leans and dips slightly, a breath lifts and stretches by about 1%, a sway is a slow weight shift. It needs a motion block in `scene.json`; idle-only sprites get one with no `kind`. The hero's two diners nod, Jiro breathes in the hero and at the FAQ, and Jiro on the bike sways. Martin asked for people to "move more", as in the reference videos.
  - **Draw order** while something moves: layers → every moving object's `under` patch → ambient sprites (a moving sprite's own ambient frame is skipped) → frame reactions → moving cut-outs. Patches go under everything else so they never cover a neighbouring sprite, and the cut-out goes on top so it can leave its box.
- A scene without effects is only redrawn when the frame signature changes (moving reactions add one key per 16 ms), and is skipped when off screen.
- **Effects scenes** keep a second canvas, `comp`, holding layers and sprites. It is repainted only when the frame signature changes; at most every 30 ms the visible canvas copies `comp` and the effects are drawn on top with `dt` measured between effect frames (capped at 0.1 s), so effects run in real time whatever the frame rate. `release` and `restore` shrink and reallocate `comp` with the visible canvas.
- Reduced motion freezes ambient sprites on frame 0 and disables moving reactions and idle moves; frame-strip reactions still play. Effects are then not animated: no `comp` is made, and they are drawn once, still, over each repaint (glows stay lit).

**Ambient effects** (`fx.ts`, `fxModel.ts`). Round 7 replaced every two-frame light sprite (lanterns, toro, the street lamp, the coin slot, the candle, the band-5 wall lantern) and the firefly, tea, cup and faucet sprites with effects drawn in code. Martin's complaint was that a light flaring looked like "a square appearing over it": a frame swap changes a whole rectangle at once. `createFx(defs, seed, reduced)` is made per scene from `scene.json` `fx` (world units, seeded from the scene id) and draws in canvas px at `G`.

| Kind | Look |
| --- | --- |
| `glow` | A radial halo added with `lighter` over the painted light, breathing ±`amp` (5%) over `period` s; `flicker` adds a small fast wobble for flames (the band-0 candle) |
| `fireflies` | Warm points with soft halos wandering inside `area`; see below |
| `ripples` | Thin elliptical rings spawned at random in `areas` every ~`every` s, growing and fading (`rippleAt`) |
| `rain`, `splash` | Slanted streaks falling through `area`; small crowns at puddle `spots` with a ring after each |
| `steam`, `drip` | Three staggered wisps rising from a point; a drop falling `fall` units with a tiny ring where it lands |
| `fish` | Dark koi shadows gliding back and forth under the water |

- `createFireflies` (pure, unit tested) steers each fly toward a target drifting on two slow sines, clamped inside its area. Every ~`swarmEvery` s (randomised) they gather (2.5 s), circle a point in a tight cluster (4 s) and scatter (3 s); `swarming()` is true while gathered or circling. No swarm appears in the logged reference frames; it is an occasional event because Martin recalls one.
- Where effects are used: glows on every painted lantern or lamp in the stops plus the band-0 candle and band-5 wall lantern; fireflies at the pond and in band 5; ripples (water and the moon's reflection) and fish at the pond; rain and splashes on the street; steam over the hero rice cooker, the compare tea cups and the FAQ cup; a drip at the band-3 faucet. The old light sprites' eggs remain as click areas.

**Frame loop and scroll** (`main.ts`).

Round 6 replaced the wheel surge and the 40 px soft settle with a magnetic scroll: Martin wanted it to be nearly impossible to rest between scenes, with the belt visibly reacting to the scroll. The page owns one `glide` at a time (from, to, start, lead, duration); `spans()` measures every built stop element, and `snap.ts` decides targets.

```
 wheel gesture ──► wheelTarget ──► glideTo(lead 250 ms) ──┐
 (touch / keys / scrollbar end) ──► glideTarget ──► glideTo(lead 0)
                                                          ▼
          journey.glide(true): belt eases to 1.5× ──► lead ──► ease-in-out scroll ──► land: journey.glide(false)
```

- **Wheel** (non-passive listener). Ctrl-zoom, mostly horizontal deltas and wheels over an element that can scroll itself (the table on phones) are left alone. A gesture starts after 250 ms of wheel silence. If `wheelTarget` is `null` the wheel scrolls natively (inside a tall stop, or at the ends). Otherwise the deltas are swallowed and accumulated until they reach 4 px (trackpads often open with a 1–2 px nudge), then one glide starts with a 0.25 s lead in which only the belt speeds up. The rest of that gesture, its inertia, anything during a glide and the 200 ms after it lands are all swallowed, so one flick moves exactly one scene.
- **Free scrolls** (touch, keyboard, scrollbar). The scroll listener records the direction and time of any scroll that is not the glide's own. Once 160 ms pass with no scroll and no wheel, no finger down (`touchstart`/`touchend`/`touchcancel`) and no scrollbar drag (a `pointerdown` at or right of `documentElement.clientWidth`, until `pointerup`), `glideTarget` is consulted and a glide with no lead starts if the page is between resting places.
- **The glide** runs in the frame loop's `settle`: after the lead it scrolls with an ease-in-out cubic over 650–1100 ms depending on distance, rounding to whole px and recording `expectY` so the scroll listener ignores its own scrolls. A user scroll during the glide (after the lead), a `touchstart` or a resize cancels it and drops the belt back to rest; a resize then re-snaps once the new layout is in. Under reduced motion there is no lead and the glide takes 1 ms, so it is a jump.
- Each frame then ticks the journey, runs `settle`, tends every stop and band scene (release, restore, visibility, draw; see Scenes) and draws the belt.

### Things to Know

- **`BUILT` still gates what is mounted.** It now lists every stop, so nothing is cut off, but `buildPageRoute` still adds an `unbuilt` rect from the bottom of the last `BUILT` stop downward. Removing a stop from `BUILT` hides the belt below it and drops its scene, band and eggs; the pond-only pieces (koi line, water, Daily Roll cabinet) assume the pond is mounted.
- **The compare and product panels break the 40% copy-field rule on purpose.** Martin asked three times for the compare panels to cover almost the whole scene, then for them to reach down to the belt; e2e asserts on desktop that they span ≥75% of the stop width and that each panel's bottom is at ≥68% of the art height and above the belt plates. He then asked for the product demo to be about 50% bigger and almost screen filling; `tests/e2e/product.spec.ts` asserts on desktop that the demo panel is ≥60% of the viewport width and ≥75% of its height, and that the product Jiro egg is ≤12% of the width and entirely right of the panel. The panel's world-unit box in `style.css` and the half-size product art have to move together: the empty left field in the art is what keeps the panel off the office.
- **The compare belt is mostly inside walls.** It enters the counter channel from inside the left wall and leaves into the right wall, so both 90° corners there sit in `HIDDEN` rects. Those rects are derived from `COMPARE_CHANNEL`, so if the compare art is regenerated or shifted again, re-measuring that one constant moves the channel and the hidden corners together. E2E asserts that plates in the compare art sit below 75% of its height.
- **The shaft, the route and the compare right-wall rect move together.** The visible right-edge run is at `R` = 320 because every scene from band 2 to band 5 paints a steel shaft there; the FAQ and street masters, and band 4, were shifted sideways in the art pipeline to put it there (`@/final/v2/art/README.md`). The compare stop's lower-right `HIDDEN` rect (x 300, 76 wide) must still cover the channel-exit corner at that x. Regenerating any shaft art means checking it against `R` rather than moving `R`.
- **The pond trestle, the koi and the hidden rects share one constant.** `POND_TRESTLE` places the last belt run, the two pond `HIDDEN` rects and `koiLine()`. If the pond art is regenerated, re-measure it and the belt, the hidden turn and the koi's arc all follow. The koi's leap offsets (45 units out, `DESCENT` 27.1 to the descent, 80 high) are tuned together: changing one moves where the koi crosses the line, so the aim at `fullestRun` would miss.
- **The game must stay out of the page's scope.** The engine has global classes, one endless rAF loop and no keyboard capture, which is why it runs in an iframe created on first click rather than being mounted into the page. Keep any new game control inside `public/games/cabinet/` and talk to it only by `postMessage`.
- **Belt ends are cut by the hidden rects, not by the route.** Because the erase uses the same rects as hiding, a `HIDDEN` rect's top edge is where the visible belt stops. Moving or resizing a rect moves the cut line. E2E checks that the belt canvas has pixels 10 px above and none 3 px below the cut at the dining-room exit (176/202 of the compare art) and at the hero bottom. Picking, rare-event candidates and the `__jiro` plate and slot lists still use the centre test `isHidden`, so a plate half-cut at a belt end cannot be grabbed once its centre is covered.
- **Never resize the belt canvas every frame.** Setting `canvas.width` or `canvas.height` clears the canvas. `resize()` only assigns the size when it actually changes, and runs only on relayout.
- **Mobile copy-height race with web fonts.** On narrow screens the layout depends on `.copy` heights, and those change when web fonts arrive. A `ResizeObserver` on every `.copy` triggers `relayout()` (layout, route rebuild, belt resize) when a height differs from the cached `copyH`. A final `relayout()` also runs after `document.fonts.ready`. Without this, the belt and egg buttons drift away from the art on phones.
- **Only one turn is not 90°.** The hand-off from the hero diagonal (slope taken from the hero art) to the vertical drop behind the crawlspace beam turns about 52°. Its whole fillet must stay inside the second `HIDDEN` rect. Every other turn is 90°. `tests/unit/route.test.ts` asserts that visible bends are 90° and that the route only travels down the page. If you re-measure the hero belt line or change `BEND_R`, keep that corner inside `HIDDEN`.
- The route is rebuilt in page px on every relayout (`buildPageRoute`), so the hidden rects and the bend radius scale with `s`.
- `bedOnly` tests page y above the bottom of the hero.
- **Resting places are stop elements, not stop tops in world units.** `spans()` reads each built stop's `offsetTop`/`offsetHeight`, so on narrow screens a stop's span includes its stacked `.copy` block and is usually taller than the screen; the page then scrolls freely through it and only snaps at its ends.
- **A cut-out and its patch must line up with frame 0.** Both cover the sprite's whole crop box and are drawn at the sprite's `x`/`y`; the patch is only opaque near the subject. If a motion sprite's crop or the base is re-fitted without rerunning `tools/motion.py`, the hidden spot and the moving object drift apart (see `@/final/v2/art/README.md`).
- **Effects and idle moves are spec data.** `fx` lives in the spec's `scene` block and `idle` on the spec's sprite, and `export-scene.py` copies both into `scene.json`; edit the spec and re-export rather than the generated file. Effect coordinates are world units, so they survive density changes like surfaces and eggs. Glow positions are measured from the painted lanterns: re-fitting a base means checking them.
- **Overrides are keyed by slot, not by plate.** A `moved` override belongs to the slot the plate was dropped on and travels with that slot. `slotsInView().filled` still counts only override-free slots, so it does not include moved plates.
- Rim colour is random per slot, not strictly alternating.
- The belt seed is fixed (`20261001`), so every visit sees the same plate stream and the same rare-event schedule.
- To cache-bust preview URLs, use `?v=`, never `?t=`. This is a standing review rule carried over from the sketch builds, where `?t=` controlled the animation clock.
- **Far-away canvases are 1 × 1.** Any test or tool that reads scene canvas sizes must look only at canvases near the viewport. `tests/e2e/compare.spec.ts`'s density tests therefore only check canvases within one screen of the viewport (and require at least two of them).
- E2E tests run serially (one worker) across Chromium and WebKit, each at a 1440 × 900 desktop and a 390 × 844 mobile viewport. Some WebKit cases are skipped by design. `tests/e2e/final.spec.ts` covers the final pass: stop order and an error-free full scroll, every FAQ question answering with its live text, the four plans readable, the price tags (pins, bullets, one CTA, Team hanging lower), the koi leaping and eating at the pond, a dropped plate feeding the koi (desktop only), no koi under reduced motion, Daily Roll in the stall (palette-only pixels, pausing off-screen), at least 54 eggs with the new stops' eggs uncovered, the hero canvas released at the pond and repainted on return, and the still page with JavaScript off. `tests/unit/koi.test.ts` covers the koi's arc, heading and schedule. `tests/unit/snap.test.ts` and `tests/unit/motion.test.ts` cover resting places and the glide speed; `tests/e2e/hero.spec.ts` checks that one wheel flick lands exactly on the next stop with the belt at about 1.5× while moving, that items are drawn upright everywhere, and that a scroll left in a band glides on to a stop. `tests/e2e/reactions.spec.ts` checks that the hero cat physically moves and leaves its spot clean, then settles back pixel-close, and that no frame-swap reactions remain except Jiro's and the traffic light's. `tests/e2e/living.spec.ts` covers round 7: no light sprites and at least 12 glows across scenes, the pond's water changing and its fireflies swarming within 45 s, rain pixels on the street, idle people (declared in `scene.json`, and the hero diners' corner changing on screen), a plate dragged along the belt riding on, a moved plate picked up and let go nowhere staying on the belt, plates resting on the street and the pond bank, and every click on a plate reacting. `tests/unit/effects.test.ts` and `tests/unit/fx.test.ts` cover the effect table, fireflies and ripples. `tests/e2e/table.spec.ts` covers stop 4: stop order, the readable table, the board's styling (no footer line, the Nori column outlined in green on both sides, four clips), the belt through the stop, the game playing inside the stop box with no dialog and palette-only pixels (it reads the iframe's canvas), the `game-rush` egg, Space scrolling the page and leaving pausing the game (desktop only), and that the stop's eggs are reachable and uncovered. The per-device density test runs at deviceScaleFactor 1 and 2 on desktop only (canvas px per CSS px must be ≥ dpr and ≤ 2 · dpr); phones keep their own device scale.
