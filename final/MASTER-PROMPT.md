You are the integration designer and engineer for Jiro.bot. Read `DESIGN-BRIEF.md`, every `research/video-NN.md`, `research/SOURCES.md`, `research/GAMES-SOURCE.md`, `research/PRODUCT-FACTS.md`, and `site/docs/CONTENT-SOURCES.md` before changing the site. The half-second logs are evidence of the reference videos; the brief and Martin's latest message are the acceptance criteria when a video shows an earlier conflicting choice. Work in `final/site/`, leaving the archived demo versions untouched.

Create a polished, calm 16-bit pixel art, seven-stop website in this order: (1) full-width inhabited bar hero, (2) focused product demo, (3) good taste versus bad taste illustrated code comparison, (4) published Nori comparison table with optional Sushi Rush, (5) FAQ, (6) transparent pricing on the rainy street, (7) pond with koi and optional Daily Roll. A single continuous physical sushi conveyor threads through every stop, originating behind the hero kitchen window and ending across the pond when the koi eats sushi. Scroll advances the scene straight down at a fixed game-observer angle. Use broad, plausible 90-degree course changes behind architectural occlusion. No orbit, pivot, cut, reverse, duplicated belt, fallen origin plates, or abrupt scene swap. Keep the belt drifting at rest and briefly boost its perceived travel with initial scroll; ease gently to one base speed.

Render all belt plates white with only a near-white or faint blue rim. Keep roughly half the plate positions empty with stable, irregular gaps. Of occupied plates, aim for 70% playful sushi food, 20% strange surprises, and 10% highly animated food. The belt, Jiro and objects use finer pixel detail than the rooms while sharing the palette in the brief. Add object-specific click feedback, a few rare physical falls, and draggable plates that persist on any authored flat surface. Show an occasional large koi leap that consumes a visible piece of sushi and restores the calm pond. Add 50 or more distinct findable Easter eggs with subtle responses, including hero props, blinking black fuzzy dust creatures, hidden paired eyes, product-desk details, street props and pond details. All essential content is readable and functional without discovery clicks.

Treat Sushi Rush and Daily Roll as the approved pair documented by the source game branch. The actual bundled `/games/arcade/` implementation must be launched by clearly labeled, optional controls in the comparison-table scene and pond scene respectively. A modal, embedded cabinet, or local game view is acceptable if it does not interrupt scroll or hide main information without an easy return. Avoid rejected whack-a-mole. Preserve their documented play and score behavior.

Use only product claims already published at https://noriagentic.com/. The product demo and good/bad terminal stories are **illustrative interactive examples**, and should say so. Keep the current published comparison rows, FAQ topics, and prices. Never replace pricing with placeholder numbers or imply the staged comparison is a real measured bake-off. CTA links should point to the published Nori flow.

The page must be visually consistent at desktop and mobile widths. Favor dark negative space behind text, readable table overflow, optional accessible interaction hints, and restrained seamless ambient loops. Use Canvas 2D and HTML/CSS, plus static image fallbacks for video assets. Keep a functioning no-WebGL/Safari-compatible route. Build locally, test the journey in a real browser, inspect all seven scenes and asset requests, exercise the two games and koi, record a short full-scroll review clip and seven scene stills. Document the exact sources and work left to polish. Do not claim a test was run if it was not.
