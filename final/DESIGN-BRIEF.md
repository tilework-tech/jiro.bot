# Jiro.bot final experience: pixel art direction

## Intent and evidence

Build one scrollable, playable restaurant world with seven readable stops: hero, product demo, good taste versus bad taste, comparison table, FAQ, pricing, and pond. The supplied ten recordings are visual references, not proof of implemented behavior. Each has an individual half-second inspection log in `research/video-NN.md`; `research/SOURCES.md` identifies the original Slack files. Preserve their strongest composition and atmosphere while applying Martin's latest written constraints when they disagree with the videos.

The point of view is a fixed three-quarter game observer. The page moves vertically on scroll. Never orbit, tilt, zoom, reverse the belt, change vanishing point, or stage a camera trick between scenes. Transitions should feel like passing through connected rooms and quiet service corridors in one building. Use beams, hatchways, noren, cabinets, street awnings, and bridge railings to hide physically necessary belt turns. A turn changes course by 90 degrees at most, over a visible broad radius. No sharp kink and no second or ghost belt.

## Art system

- Polished 16-bit pixel art, coherent perspective and consistent scale. Treat the room as a 2 to 4 pixel block grid at desktop reference size, with two or three shade steps per material. Do not blur pixel silhouettes or mix photorealistic cutouts into the world.
- Dark base: ink `#140d09`, walnut `#241510`, cherry `#3b1f1c`, charcoal `#1e1814`. Warm light: cream `#f6ecdc`, paper `#d9c9b0`, copper `#e0894c`, toasted rail `#a3582f`. Quiet accents: indigo `#27325c`, sage `#576a58`, cyan `#5fd4ff`, koi coral `#dc795c`. Restrict saturated cyan to Jiro's eyes and product highlights. Food can add controlled salmon, tuna, roe, tamago and wasabi color.
- Draw Jiro, the conveyor, its plates, and belt objects at about twice the detail density of background architecture, with crisper edges and extra shading. Keep their palette bound to the same room colors. Jiro is a meticulous sushi-master robot and an opinionated senior engineer. No drawn mouth. The jaw grille can move a few pixels while speaking. In the product scene, eyes are cyan shapes without pupils.
- Restaurant spaces should feel lived in: diners, stacked crockery, bottles, cloth, paper lanterns and kitchen tools. Keep copy and product UI against broad, low-detail dark fields. Let each stop have a busy edge and a calm reading zone.
- Ambient movement at rest changes under roughly 5% of a scene at once. Let lantern light breathe, cloth lift, eyes blink, steam rise, dust drift, and Jiro make minute hand or jaw motions. Loops must return to exactly the starting pose with no cut or speed jump. The belt continues a gentle crawl while the page is still.
- Put small black fuzzy dust creatures in dark corners and transition niches. Most sit and blink. Some respond to a click with a tiny hop or a carried rice grain. Avoid wall mice. Use inspired creature behavior without importing unlicensed film art.
- A shared typography system pairs a legible pixel display face for short headings with a readable sans face for body, tables, cards, and controls. Copy stays concise. All essential product information remains usable without clicking an Easter egg.

## Conveyor choreography and objects

The belt is one path in document space, with one persistent ordered stream of plate identities. Camera scroll reveals successive stretches of it. Plates in the hero are the very same plate stream seen later. Keep the rail walnut/copper and the slatted bed charcoal so it fits all rooms. Every plate is white, with only a faint white or icy-blue rim; no red, green, yellow, black or multicolored rims. At a representative instant, only about half of available plate positions carry objects. Empty intervals vary through a seeded, stable pattern, including singles and small groups, and do not reshuffle in view.

Among occupied plates, target 70% funny sushi/food-related objects, 20% surprising nonfood or unusual objects, and 10% distinctly animated food. Examples: expressive nigiri and onigiri, rice with a tiny umbrella, suspicious wasabi, miniature lucky cat, rubber duck, floppy disk, small beetle, and rare maki with legs that crosses one plate gap to cuddle another. Use weighted categories, not a tidy repeating sequence. Keep each item small enough that the plate remains readable. Very rare slips or collisions can send an object off the belt, with a short physical fall and rest or return; never teleport it.

At rest the belt and plates share one constant direction and gentle speed. On initial user scroll, increase the apparent plate travel briefly relative to the scene, then ease back to normal when scrolling stops, without abrupt acceleration or a reverse. The stream remains continuous across hidden wall passages. Plate click actions depend on the object: a puffer expands, wasabi protests, tea steams, a fortune reveals a note, an expressive onigiri reacts, and animated food can hop. Dragging a plate to a visible flat counter or table in any scene with enough surface leaves it there until a meaningful later user action; dropping in the pond makes a splash. Mouse, touch, and keyboard equivalents should be clear where practical. Place a few clickable secrets within the hero.

## Discoverable detail

Provide over 50 distinct, deterministic Easter egg identities across props, creatures, belt objects, games and pond. A unique identity should correspond to a visible object or discoverable action and a perceivable response, not 50 variations of one click. Keep the collection unobtrusive. Suggested distribution: 8 hero props, 7 service nook creatures and tools, 7 product desk objects, 7 comparison room objects, 7 chart passage objects, 6 FAQ props, 7 street/pricing objects, 8 pond objects, plus a few item behaviors. Distinguish earned events in a small counter or discovery list. Do not place secrets over body copy or primary controls.
