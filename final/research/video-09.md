## Scene design and composition

**Observed:** This sample illustrates one specific transition from a sushi bar hero through an intermediate underfloor niche to the product demo. The framing resembles a side-view, vertically scrolling pixel-art game, with light isometric cues in the countertop and belt. It does not spin or orbit. The left side is reserved for sparse copy or atmosphere in the hero, while the robot and diners anchor the busy right side. In the product room, the dominant central rectangle is the monitor; Jiro and the shelves sit outside its reading area. The empty black space at the belt turn gives a visual breath before the detailed underfloor room.

**Observed:** The art uses crisp stepped pixel edges and moderate hand-drawn shading: near-black, dark walnut, toasted orange, copper, mauve, muted sage-green, and cyan eye highlights. Lighting is a warm bar glow above, a small flickering-looking amber pool around the creatures in the middle, and a low teal monitor-room glow below. No pale page background appears. Decorative rooms use dark, low-contrast texture so bright food, eyes, buttons, and screen text remain the focal points.

**Observed:** The belt has near-black moving surface, dark grey segment divisions, raised reddish-brown rails and a broad diagonal run in the hero. Its gentle leftward bend sends it through a floor opening to a narrower vertical form in the next rooms. Each plate appears in a box-like compartment on the vertical segment. The source seems visually continuous by position and theme, but the recording does not prove that a single physical conveyor mesh or shared stream is implemented. The foreground floor/beam partly hides its passage, which is useful for making a necessary bend plausible. In this reference, plate rims are many colors and the belt is much more than half full. Those two observed choices conflict with the new brief's white or faint blue rims and roughly half-filled, random stream.

**Observed:** The soot-like creatures are three discrete black round fuzzy figures with white eyes, not wall mice. They inhabit the dim service space without occupying the path of the belt. Their candle creates a tiny local focus, with a few pale particles around them. They are not visibly interacted with in this file. A smiling onigiri, suspicious green blob, grey figurine, cat figurine, and other nonstandard plates show how surprise objects can sit within a largely sushi-oriented setting. These are examples visible in the reference, not proof of implemented click effects or item behaviors.
