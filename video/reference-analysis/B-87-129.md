## Techniques

- **Story structure: a "person, their bot, what it does" anthology.** Each employee gets a short vignette: a person on camera, a lower-third naming their bot, then macro shots of the prompt being typed and the agent's result. Within this range: Notion Bot (~44–47s), Simon's Bot "Cartman" / LinkedIn research (~47–55s), an email-drafting beat (~55.5–59s), a human reaction and conversation (~59.5–63s), and the start of Krista's Bot "Olive" (63.5s).
- **Prompt, then result, as a hard-cut pair.** A typed prompt ("Can you please navigate to my Linkedin…", "Draft emails to all 100 lea…") is shown for about 1–1.5s and cut before it finishes. The next shot is already the outcome (LinkedIn open, a list of drafted emails). The viewer's brain completes the sentence, and the action feels instant.
- **Pacing.** Most shots last 1–2.5s. Product shots last about 1.5–5s (the LinkedIn agent sequence is the longest at ~4.5s). Human B-roll runs 1–2s. Hard cuts throughout; the only transitions are in-camera pans and push-ins. No wipes or graphic transitions.
- **Product UI is filmed, not screen-captured.** Real displays are shot off-axis with a cinema lens: shallow depth of field, rack focus, slow dolly/push, chromatic aberration, a visible keyboard and bezel. No device-frame mockups and no flat screen recordings. The grade is consistent (teal/mint highlights, warm skin, crushed blacks), which makes ordinary UI (Notion, LinkedIn, Gmail) feel cinematic and premium.
- **Clarity despite the blur.** Focus falls only on the one element that matters (the input text, the "Open" pill, the page title). Typography in the chat composer is very large in frame (the macro makes it read like a headline). Small UI captions such as "Notion Bot's screen" and "Harry's screen" label whose agent view this is.
- **Agent cursor as a character.** An oversized black arrow cursor with a motion trail moves across LinkedIn, so the viewer sees that the agent is operating the browser itself. Hover pills ("⤢ Open") show interactivity.
- **Lower-thirds.** Bottom-left, no background plate, clean sans (Inter-like). Line 1 "Name's Bot: ● BotName" in white at regular/medium weight. Line 2 is a short benefit statement, lighter and grey. They fade in ~0.5s after the cut, persist across cuts (bridging product shot and person, ~5.5s), and slide/fade out. Copy is 3–5 words of benefit ("Researches like you would", "Contextually generates customer materials").
- **Human warmth between product beats.** Interview two-shots, laughter, a close-up profile, and hands on a keyboard make it a story about people with agents rather than a feature list. No on-screen narration text beyond the lower-thirds; the story is presumably carried by interview audio.
- **Scale shown, not stated.** "100 leads" in the prompt, then a cut to a monitor of repeated drafted email rows.
- **Color as chaptering.** The Notion/LinkedIn beat is cool mint and daylight, the email beat is dark mode with red bokeh, the conversation is warm tungsten, and Krista's beat is cool teal. Each grade shift signals a new vignette.
