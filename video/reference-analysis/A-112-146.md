## Techniques

- **This is not a designed hero video.** The range is a casual screen capture of OpenAI's "Introducing dots" launch film playing muted inside the article. The useful lessons come from the embedded film. The capture wrapper (Safari chrome, hover overlay, pause glyph) adds noise and should not be copied.
- **Storytelling: show, don't tell.** The film carries a single thread: the agent proposes work ("caught the app migration in your Google Meet notes. I've already mapped out the changes."), the human approves with a constraint ("Get it started, but make sure… first check every flow against the current product"), the agent acknowledges (thumbs-up plus typing dots), and then the agent visibly acts on its own computer (cursor clicking through a real website and booking a consultation slot). The human reacts with delight, the same agent follows her to her phone, and the film closes on a calm exit. This is an approve-then-delegate loop, and the agent never needs explaining.
- **The "agent's computer" device.** A titled frame, "Jojo's Computer", on a saturated brand-purple background holds a cropped browser card with an oversized cursor plus the mascot riding along with it. This makes it clear that the agent is driving and that it is a computer of its own. This idea maps directly to Jiro working in its own cloud session or terminal.
- **Pacing.** Shots last about 1.5 to 2.5 s each (close-up 2 s, wide 2 s, agent-computer 2 s, reaction 1 s, face 1.5 s, phone 2.5 s, exterior 2.5 s+). The rhythm alternates screen close-up → human wide → screen → human face. Each UI beat holds only long enough to read one message or see one click.
- **Transitions.** Mostly hard cuts on action. One foreground body wipe (0133) moves from interior to exterior. There are no flashy digital transitions.
- **Framing of the product UI.** It is never a flat screenshot. The UI is filmed on physical screens (wall display, phone) over the shoulder with shallow depth of field, and a blurred head or hair in the foreground partly occludes the text. The UI is very large in frame, with chat bubbles at about 1/10 of frame height, so the type is legible at a glance. The camera makes slow push-ins and drifts.
- **Typography and color.** The UI text is large, rounded sans. The agent uses grey bubbles and the user uses saturated magenta. The brand palette is magenta/purple plus a playful blue blob mascot with eyes, set against warm wood and neutral light. The site-in-site uses an elegant serif ("Wedding cakes, composed for you.") to show a real-world task.
- **Characterization.** The agent has a name ("Jojo") and a mascot that appears across surfaces (wall screen, browser cursor, phone). Consistent identity across devices sells "always within reach". The Jiro sushi-robot could fill the same role.
- **Captions/narration.** There are no burned-in captions. Story text lives inside the UI itself. The film plays muted here, and the story still reads. A good hero video should work with sound off.
- **Cursor.** The real user's cursor is irrelevant (it only triggers the player overlay). The in-film agent cursor is the hero: oversized, deliberate, gliding to targets and producing visible state changes (modal opens, slot selected).
- **Ending.** The recording ends on a paused, dark, low-contrast frame with no logo, end card, or CTA. That is a weak ending caused by the capture. The film's own ending (dusk exterior, silhouette walking away, "the agent keeps working while you live your life") is the emotional close. For a hero loop, end on a resolved product state plus wordmark, or loop seamlessly.
- **Premium feel.** It comes from cinematic lighting (warm practicals, bokeh), real human emotion (laughing close-up), physical sets rather than screen recordings, restraint (one idea per shot), and a brand color repeated across UI, set, and mascot.
