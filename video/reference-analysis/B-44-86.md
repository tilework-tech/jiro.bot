# Video B, frames f_0044 to f_0086 (t=21.5s to 42.5s)

This is not a screen recording. It is a cinematic live-action brand film: shallow depth of field, a teal/green screen glow against warm amber/wood interiors, and a slight letterbox (thin black bar at the top). The structure is "person + their bot". Each person gets a two-line lower-third that names the bot and says what it does. B-roll alternates between faces (reactions) and extreme macro shots of the product UI on a laptop screen.

Lower-third format: line 1 is white, medium weight, "<Name>'s Bot: ● <BotName>", with a small white circle glyph (the logo) before the bot name. Line 2 is white, regular weight, slightly smaller, and states the capability. Both lines are left-aligned around x=10% and y=82%. There is no box or shadow; the text sits straight on the footage. The lower-third persists across several cuts and changes only when the next character's segment starts.

## Techniques

- **Storytelling structure:** A repeated three-to-four-shot pattern per character: (1) face/reaction, (2) lower-third naming "X's Bot: ● Name" plus one capability line, (3) one or two extreme macro shots of the product doing exactly that capability, (4) back to a face. Capabilities escalate: triage email (volume), learn workflows by demonstration (teachability), work in the cloud on its own (autonomy), then a scheduled recurring digest (proactivity). Each bot has a cute personal name, which makes the bots feel like personalities and teammates rather than features.
- **Pacing:** Character segments last about 5 to 10 s (Yodo about 2 s visible in this range, Louie about 10 s from 25.0 to 35.0, Bobby about 4.5 s from 35.5 to 40.0, plus 40.5 onward). Individual shots hold 1 to 4 s. Faces hold 1 to 1.5 s. UI macros hold 2 to 4 s, long enough to read one sentence. Typing is shown in real time over 2.5 s, then cut before it finishes. Short unlabeled "breather" shots (library rack focus 23.5 to 24.5, hand on mouse 35.0, bearded man 40.5) separate segments.
- **Transitions:** All hard cuts; no wipes or dissolves. Motion comes from the camera instead: constant slow push-ins, lateral drifts, and a rack focus. The lower-third fades in about 0.5 s after the first shot of each segment and is absent during transitional shots.
- **Product UI framing:** Never a flat screen recording. It is always shot through the lens on a real laptop, at steep oblique angles, with very shallow depth of field so only one line or button is sharp. Physical-device cues (bezel corner with green camera LED, screen pixel grain, rainbow reflection, out-of-focus foreground hands/shoulders) make it feel tangible and premium. Crops are aggressive: one input box, one button ("Teach a task", "Learn from demonstration"), one bot message. The viewer sees exactly one idea per shot.
- **Making it clear what you're looking at:** The lower-third states the capability in plain words, and the very next macro shows a literal UI string that matches it ("Learns and replicates tricky workflows" becomes "Watch me respond to emails and mimic…", then "Teach a task", then "Learn from demonstration", then "Got it — watching the demo and turning it into a skill."). Cause and effect is shown in sequence: click "Teach a task" and a red "■ 0:00" recording pill and red window border appear.
- **Cursor use:** Minimal and real. A hand-pointer hovers then clicks a single button, and once a remote-desktop icon is clicked, a click ripple shows. No synthetic zoom-on-cursor.
- **Typography:** Lower-third in a clean geometric sans (similar to Inter or Suisse), white. The name line is about 24 px at 1100 px width (about 2.2% of frame width), medium weight. The descriptor is a touch lighter and smaller. The "●" logo glyph is inline. No background plate, low and left, unobtrusive. UI copy is short, warm, first-person bot voice ("Got it —", "You're set.", "I'll keep watch. Rest easy.").
- **Color/grade:** A cohesive teal/mint grade. Screens glow mint green, which matches the product UI palette, against warm amber/wood/skin tones in the people shots, giving complementary teal-orange cinema contrast. Blacks are lifted slightly and the image has film-like softness.
- **Captions/narration:** No subtitles in this range. The faces appear to be speaking (testimonial/interview style), so audio likely carries the narrative. The only on-screen text overlays are the lower-thirds.
- **What feels premium:** Real people and real offices; cinema glass with bokeh; restraint (one message per shot, no UI annotations, arrows, or callout boxes); recurring cast across scenes; and product details that read as authentic real-world usage (Slack workspace names, Salesforce dashboards, "Stan's screen", Notion, Gmail).
