## Techniques

- **Story structure, a repeating "person → bot → proof" unit.** Each vignette is anchored by a named human and their named bot ("Krista's Bot: Olive", "Emma's Bot: Cat"). It moves through: human close-up → screen macro of the request → agent status messages → visible output → human reaction (laugh) → next vignette. One plain capability claim per vignette in the caption.
- **Agents shown as coworkers.** Bots have human first names (Josh, Stan, Sarah, Lauren, Dorothy...) and colorful 3D blob avatars with eyes. Bot-to-bot handoff in a group chat ("Handed the words to Copywriter.", "Yep — Copywriter the page shell is ready...") turns "multi-agent" into a scene you can watch.
- **Concrete, specific tasks.** Granola notes → Notion → Japanese end-of-meeting deck. "Brian's Lemonade" website. These use real-sounding integrations (Gmail, Granola, Notion, Slack) and file paths ("/workspace/brian-lemonade/index.html"). Agent messages are written in a first-person, casual, progress-narrating voice.
- **A visible before/after payoff.** "Mobile App" is selected → deleted → "モバイルアプリ". The edit happens in place, which pays off the request typed seconds earlier.
- **Pacing.** Shots last 0.5 to 3.5 s. Product macros hold about 1.5 to 2 s, a thread readout up to about 3.5 s. Human reactions last about 1 s. Caption beats run about 10 s per bot (Olive about 64 to 68 s, Cat from 82 s on). All transitions are hard cuts. The only fades are on the lower-third caption.
- **Framing the product UI.** There is no device frame or flat screen capture. The UI is filmed: real laptop or phone screens shot at an oblique angle with a macro lens, very shallow depth of field (only one line of text sharp), handheld drift, slow push-ins, rack focus, and chromatic fringing. Fingers sometimes blur through the foreground. Readable text is limited to one key sentence per shot and the rest falls off into blur. That steers the eye without arrows or zooms.
- **Color.** The whole piece has a mint/teal-green cast (UI shots are tinted pale green). Human shots are warm (orange bokeh, skin tones), and the grade is cinematic with lifted blacks. Accent colors come from the bot avatars (red, blue, teal, orange).
- **Typography.** The lower third is a clean geometric sans in white with no background box. Line 1 is a name plus bot glyph, line 2 a short claim. It is understated but large enough to read. The in-UI text itself carries the narrative (agent status lines).
- **Cursor and typing.** A real hand cursor picks bots from a dropdown. Real typing appears letter by letter ("Look at my Gran" → "Look at my Granola notes and g"). Nothing is overlaid or animated in post.
- **Making clear what you are looking at.** The caption names the bot and its job. Agent messages state intent ("Building a short Japanese end-of-meeting deck..."). Every UI cut is a close-up on the one relevant element.
- **Ending.** None in this range: it is a recording cut at 1:25 of a 1:45 video. The final shots move into a couch-interview testimonial two-shot, so the video likely closes on testimonials or a logo after 1:25.
- **What makes it premium.** Documentary-style human footage mixed with macro product cinematography. Real people react (laughing). The product is filmed, not screen-recorded. Restraint: no motion graphics, no voiceover text, tiny captions.
- **For a Jiro hero.** Use named-bot vignettes, the request → agent status → in-place result payoff, bot-to-bot handoff in a group thread, macro and angled UI with shallow focus, and one plain caption claim per beat.
